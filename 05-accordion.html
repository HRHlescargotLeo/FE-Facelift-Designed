Component 03 · Design layer

Accordion

Layout is unchanged from the signed-off wireframe: index on the left with every title visible, content panel on the right, one section selected at a time, stacking to a conventional accordion on mobile.

Content is the existing “Who we are” copy. Click through the index in each route — arrow keys move between sections too.

Route A

Editorial rule

Closest to the site today

Keeps the hairline rules and condensed headings the accordion uses now, but replaces the stacked drawers with the index. The selected item takes a Merlot left rule — the vertical line device the brand guidelines reserve for highlighting key information.

White surface Merlot — selected marker Midnight — headings Off-white — index hover
Desktop · Who we are

More about Frontier

Founded in 1999 by economists who wanted to work on the most interesting problems, for the most interesting clients. 1

The bedrock of our business is that we are owned entirely by our staff. This means everyone is driven to deliver the highest quality work, with an independent mind, for clients we really care about.

People like what we do, and our business has grown. The problems we solve change constantly, but our values do not. Our clients come back again and again, and many of our founders still work within the business.

How we created Frontier

A workforce spread across nine countries, and a workplace we want everyone to be able to be themselves in.

We want to reflect and embrace the power of diversity and cultural difference in everything we do for our clients and through our internal work.

We are an inclusive workplace where people are free to be who they want to be. We will be bold, visible and proactive to make sure that Frontier is and continues to be a workplace that embraces diversity.

Our D&I initiatives

We work with clients all over the world, from offices in Amsterdam, Berlin, Brussels, Cologne, Dublin, Madrid, London, Paris and Prague.

We’ve grown through recessions, booms and busts and count some of the most admired organisations in the world among our long-standing clients.

We believe in strong client relationships, have a passion for understanding the truth and employ people who want to make a difference.

Three things shape how we work, whoever the client and whatever the question.

Uncompromising thinking — we deliver exceptional work with integrity and objectivity.

Looking beyond the obvious — we think more widely than the immediate question, and deliver more than expected.

A refreshing experience — we’re smart and friendly people who communicate complex ideas with clarity.

  • 1The lead sentence is set larger with a Merlot vertical rule. It gives the panel a clear entry point and uses an existing brand element rather than inventing a new one. Everything below it is ordinary rich text, so editors are not learning a new structure.
  • Selection is marked three ways — contrasting surface, left rule and an arrow — so it never depends on colour alone.
  • Panel height is content-led here, with no internal scrolling. That is one of the open decisions in the brief; the alternative is a minimum height so the frame stops resizing between sections. Easier to judge once the final copy lengths are known.
Route B

Colour block

Boldest

The whole component is a Midnight Blue block with the index reversed out of it. The selected section brightens rather than lightening, and Banana Yellow marks it. Turns a utility pattern into a feature band on the page.

  • 2The call to action uses the existing style picker — shown here as the solid variant. On a dark surface only the solid and the white outline options are legible, so the picker would need its available styles constraining per theme, which is a development question worth raising now.
  • Rich text on a dark surface is the risk in this route. Editors paste links, bold and lists; all of those need a dark-surface treatment defined, or the first pasted link will come through in default blue.
  • Body text is white at 11.9:1. Long passages reversed out are harder to read than dark-on-light regardless of ratio, so this route suits shorter panels.
Route C

Soft tint

Closest to the admired reference

A Grey Sky tinted index against a white panel, rounded as a single unit. The lightest-touch of the three and the most comfortable for long passages of copy.
